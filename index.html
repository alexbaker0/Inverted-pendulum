How it works

The world. A 1 m rod on a frictional pivot, riding on a 1 kg cart. The full nonlinear rigid-body equations are integrated with Runge–Kutta at 400 Hz. The motor can push at most 15 N, the rail ends at ±2.4 m, and hitting an end stop jolts the pole.

The agent sees six numbers: cart position and speed, the pole angle (as sine and cosine), its spin, and the force it applied last time. It outputs one number, the motor force. That is a 6→64→64→1 network with tanh units.

The reward, each 1/50 s, is between 0 and 1. It is 1 only when the pole is upright, the cart is in the middle of the rail, nothing is moving and the motor is idle. Hitting an end stop costs 20 and ends the episode.

Training used PPO for 36 million steps, about 8 simulated days, starting from random angles, spins and positions. The physics was jittered and the cart was shoved at random, so the agent copes with a world it has not seen.

The double pendulum has two 0.6 m rods hinged end to end, with friction at both joints and a 25 N motor. Its equations come from the Lagrangian of the cart and both rods. With one motor and two joints it is chaotic and underactuated. The agent sees nine numbers: both angles as sine and cosine, both spins, plus the cart and its last push. It uses a bigger 9→128→128→1 network, trained the same way for 150 million steps (about 35 simulated days). Swing-up only clicked after about 39 million steps.
